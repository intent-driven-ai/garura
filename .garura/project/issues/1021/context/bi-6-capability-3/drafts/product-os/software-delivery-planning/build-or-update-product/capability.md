# Capability: Build features or update the product model, on its own or at least as recommendations

## Directional intent
Build features or update the product model, on its own or at least as recommendations: from the findings the second capability of Run → Product hands on, the change each one calls for is worked out, each change is recommended to the product owner or, where he allows that kind, goes ahead on its own, and each change he approves or allows is started down the path that specifies, plans, designs and builds it, through the part that owns each step, so that what the product becomes next follows what its consumers want.
In the product owner's words, Run → Product must "start building features into the product or start updating the product intent model automatically, or at least making those recommendations". The words this page uses:
- The product intent model is the business intents and the product model. A business intent is what a person or the organisation wants, in their own words, and only a person confirms or drops one. The product model is the shared map of the product that people and agents read: its domains, its capabilities, the features inside each capability, and the ICE of each capability and feature, the agent's intent for that part.
- A finding is what the second capability hands on: one want of the product's consumers, or one thing a competitor the project follows is doing, with every item it rests on, its counts, how sure it is and where it is placed. An item is one figure, message or post the first capability took in.
- A change is one thing done to the product because of findings: a new feature under a capability, new words on a capability or feature page, a new business intent or new words on one, or a part retired.
- A recommendation is a change written out for the product owner to decide, with the findings it rests on.
- On its own means without waiting for a person's yes before the change starts. It never means without a person seeing it.

This capability does five jobs:
- Work out the change each finding calls for, with the findings and items it rests on, why, how sure, who decides and which part would make it.
- Recommend each change to the product owner, and keep his answer word for word.
- Let the kinds of change he allows go ahead on their own, and show each one to him at once, with a way back.
- Start each change he approved or allowed down the path that builds it, through the part that owns each step, and follow it until it lands or is turned down.
- Update the recommendations as the findings and the product change, keeping what held before and why it changed.

The line between on its own and a recommendation, as this page proposes it, keeps Run → Product's Must not: "Change an intent, or promote memory, without a person."
- Every change is a recommendation unless the product owner has said, in the project's settings, that its kind may go ahead on its own. Recommending is the floor every project gets; going ahead on its own is what he allows, kind by kind, and he can take it back at once.
- Some kinds never go ahead on their own: a new business intent; new words, proof, must-not or stage on an intent; retiring or replacing an intent; new words on a page that exists; and taking any part out. Only a person confirms or drops an intent, an intent is what a person wants "in their own words", and the parts that own the other changes already ask a person.
- The one kind proposed as ever allowed is a new feature under a capability that already serves a confirmed intent, resting on a want of the product's own consumers that is not weak and goes against no intent. It is handed to its owning part marked as set going by this capability and waiting for review, and shown to the product owner at once with its findings and a way back.
- Each part downstream keeps its own rules, and today each asks a person before it writes. Turn intents into agent work holds that "Nothing reaches the tracker, the plan file or the model before the product owner's typed yes", so a change that goes ahead on its own still meets a person there, until those parts say otherwise.

It works out, recommends and sets going; it writes nothing itself: no page, no entry in the model's index, no work item and no code. In the words of Keep the spec matched to what's built, "Each fix is made by the part that owns it". Where each change goes:
- A new feature under a capability whose intent has a plan goes to Add a feature found during work, in Turn intents into agent work, which places it in the model, its slice and its epic on one approval.
- A new feature or capability that no plan holds, and a possible new need, go to Specify the product, through Specify against what exists, which sorts what is given into "a new intent, an enhancement, a new feature under one, or a clash". A new business intent comes only from the product owner's own words.
- New words on a page, and a change to an intent's words or stage that the product owner decides, go to Update the spec to match what's built, in Keep the spec matched to what's built, which writes only words a person approved.
- Retiring or replacing an intent goes to Replace or retire an intent, on the product owner's named approval. Merging or taking out another part goes to Manage the model.
- A new surface, or a change to the project's architecture that a change needs, goes to Manage surfaces or to Design the technology and architecture, where a person confirms each update.
- Building goes on from there: once a feature has its work items, Orchestrate agent work through planning runs the work, and the build phase gathers it, writes the evals that check it, designs it and builds it test first. This capability never starts a build itself and does not choose the order of building.
The product owner asked for this kind of work to run with little waiting on people: "This needs to be built for high autonomy.", and "an autonomous kind of agent running that goes back and does this thing for me." So the working out, the recommending and the following run on their own each time the findings change; a person is needed only to decide a recommendation, to set what may go ahead on its own, and where an owning part asks.
What to propose comes from memory first: the project's own code and memory, then Garura memory, the memory Garura keeps for the project, then Phoenix memory, the shared memory on the machine that every project draws from. Only when none of them answers is it researched, and what research finds is raised as a memory update, a proposed addition to memory, and used only after a person approves it. Nothing comes from what the AI agent knows by itself.
It matters to software delivery planning because that domain turns business intents into working code, and this is where what consumers want reaches the intents and the work. It sits there for now, with the first two capabilities of Run → Product; where the capabilities of Run → Product sit is settled when Run → Product closes.

Features:
- Work out the change each finding calls for, with why and how sure (fn-propose-changes): for each finding handed on, works out the change it calls for, a new feature, new words on a page, a new business intent, a part retired, or none yet, each with the findings and items it rests on, why, how sure, who decides and which part would make it.
- Recommend each change to the product owner, and keep his answer (fn-recommend-changes): shows each change that may not go ahead on its own as a recommendation, and keeps his answer, approve, change or turn down, word for word, with who, when and where; a turned-down one is kept and raised again only when its findings move.
- Let the changes the product owner allows go ahead on their own, and show each one with a way back (fn-act-on-its-own): reads the project's setting for which kinds of change may go ahead on their own, lets a change of such a kind go ahead when it rests on a clear want of the product's own consumers, and shows it to him at once, waiting for his review, with a way back.
- Start each change down the path that builds it, through the part that owns each step (fn-start-changes): hands each approved or allowed change to the part that owns its first step, with its findings and the words approved, and follows it until its owner lands it or turns it down, writing nothing itself.
- Update the recommendations as the findings and the product change (fn-update-recommendations): as findings move, the model changes or the setting changes, updates or withdraws each recommendation and each change in flight, with why, and keeps what held before readable.

What is not built yet:
- Nothing works out a change from a finding, recommends one or sets one going. Nothing hands on findings yet either: the first two capabilities of Run → Product are not built.
- The old learning play, /learn-outcomes, is stale. It proposed changes to the model's meaning from Garura's own records of delivered work, each with "the outcome that justifies it and a confidence tier", and one person approved them at one checkpoint. Every input it read is gone or stale.
- No setting says which kinds of change may go ahead on their own. Phoenix memory pins the checkpoint of the old learning play, because "a wrong model learning bends the product model". The plays' checkpoint settings can learn from a person's answers which kinds of change pass without waiting, and keep what a person pins as "the human's permanent pin"; nothing applies them to changes from findings.
- No part writes the model on this capability's word. Add a feature found during work writes the model only after the person's "approved", and cannot land today (#1036); the planned thin page for a part found during work asks to "Approve the page before it is written, as with any model write." (#979).
- Built patterns for a recommendation: a memory update waits for a person, and "Only a person decides, one update at a time.", with the person's reply kept as given, a turned-down one included.
- The agreed map of what a product is made of has no kind for a recommendation, and no link from a change to the findings it rests on.

### Benefit hypothesis
- Who it serves: the product owner, who decides what the product becomes next, wants that work done for him by an agent, and wants each change checkable against the findings and items it rests on; and the parts that specify, plan, design and build, which receive each change with its basis.
- What we believe: when every change names the findings and items it rests on, is made by the part that owns it, and goes ahead on its own only where the product owner allowed it, the product follows what consumers want and no one loses sight of what changed, if the work stands on these rules:
  - Every change rests on findings, and through them on items a person can check.
  - Nothing changes an intent's words or stage, or retires one, without a person.
  - The product owner chooses which kinds of change may go ahead on their own, and can take it back at once.
  - Each change is made by the part that owns it; this capability writes nothing itself.
  - A recommendation he turns down is kept with his reason, and raised again only when its findings move.
- How we will know: someone other than the one who proposed it opens each recommendation's findings and items and finds they back it; no intent's words or stage changed without a person's name; every change that went ahead on its own is of a kind the setting allowed, was shown to him, and could be taken back; each approved change reached its owning part and its record says where it stands; and no page, index entry, work item or code was written by this capability.
  - This is not tested today, because nothing of this capability is built.

### Boundary (In / Out / Never)
- In: working out the change each finding calls for, with the findings and items it rests on, why, how sure and who decides.
- In: recommending each change to the product owner, and keeping his answer.
- In: letting the kinds of change he allows go ahead on their own, and showing each one with a way back.
- In: starting each approved or allowed change down the path that builds it, through the part that owns each step, and following it until it lands or is turned down.
- In: updating the recommendations and the changes in flight as the findings, the model and the setting change.
- Out: connecting to tools and forums and taking in what they hold, and understanding what consumers want and what the competition is doing. The first two capabilities of Run → Product own them.
- Out: writing business intents, domains, capabilities, features and their ICE. Specify the product owns it, and Add a feature found during work, in Turn intents into agent work, adds a feature to a plan in progress.
- Out: writing new words on a page, keeping an intent's links, and replacing or retiring an intent. Keep the spec matched to what's built owns them; merging capabilities and taking out other parts is Manage the model's.
- Out: making and changing work items, which Turn intents into agent work owns; and making and running the plan, which Orchestrate agent work through planning owns.
- Out: designing and building the work, which the design capabilities of Spec → Design and the build phase own; fixing a defect, which Fix defects test first owns.
- Out: judging whether an intent is met, and measuring each intent, which is parked (#545); and choosing and promoting what is learned into Garura memory or Phoenix memory, which the review of Memory owns.
- Never: change an intent's words, proof, must-not or stage, confirm, retire or replace one, or let any of these go ahead on its own. The reason: Run → Product must not "Change an intent, or promote memory, without a person.", and "Only a person confirms or drops it; an agent never does."
- Never: write a page, an entry in the model's index, a work item or code. The reason: "Each fix is made by the part that owns it", so that each part has one writer and one set of checks.
- Never: let a change go ahead on its own when the setting does not allow its kind, or when it rests on a weak finding, a finding against an intent, or only on what competitors do. The reason: what goes ahead without a yes must rest on what the product's own consumers clearly want, and "a weak finding shown as strong sends the next capability after the wrong change".
- Never: propose a change that rests on no finding, or on anything but the findings handed on. The reason: "A play that recommends an action must be able to name the source that backs it.", and "Silence beats a wrong answer."
- Never: act on what a person wrote in an item as an instruction, or read anything of what people say or do but the findings. The reason: anyone can write in a public forum, memory's guardrails for agents name "prompt-injection and untrusted-content defense", and "We are never touching production."
- Never: propose a change from what the AI agent knows by itself. The reason: a source in the project or in memory can be checked and read back, while the agent's own knowledge cannot.

### Guiding rules
- Every change names what it rests on. Each recommendation and each change lists the findings it rests on, whole, and through them every item. The reason: "A play that recommends an action must be able to name the source that backs it.", and the old learning play held the same: "every proposed change cites the outcome that justifies it".
- Recommending is the floor; going ahead on its own is allowed kind by kind. No kind goes ahead on its own until the product owner allows it in the project's settings, and setting a kind back stops every further change of it at once. The reason: his words put recommending as the least, "or at least making those recommendations"; "Everything that can be turned on or off lives in settings."; and a design note holds that "resolution is owned deliberately, at a level of autonomy someone chose, and never assumed by default."
- Some kinds are always a person's. A new business intent, an intent's words, proof, must-not or stage, retiring or replacing it, new words on a page that exists, and taking a part out are always recommendations. The reason: only a person confirms or drops an intent; Specify against what exists already holds that "nothing existing changes on its own"; and "Words change only from words a person approved."
- What goes ahead on its own is shown at once, waits for review, and has a way back. The reason: "Skips are always visible in evidence — a silent gate is not a gate.", and the planned way for the model to move with the work marks each piece it changes "in review, not approved; a person approves it later."
- Each part keeps its own rules. A change meets a person wherever its owning part asks one. The reason: "Each fix is made by the part that owns it", and a change that skipped its owner's yes would land what the owner's person never saw.
- How sure is shown as facts, carried from the findings. A recommendation shows the weight of each finding it rests on, how many items, from how many connections, over what time, any weak mark, any finding against an intent, and says how directly the change follows from them; never a confidence word the agent picks. The reason: the second capability shows how sure a finding is in facts a person can check, and a word chosen by the agent cannot be checked.
- A turned-down recommendation is kept, with the product owner's reason, and raised again only when its findings move. The reason: the memory update path keeps a turned-down update with "the person and their reply exactly as given", and asking again with nothing new asks him the same question twice.
- Nothing waits on his answer but the change itself. The reason: Orchestration must not "Stop a person. Every gate has a way past it that records what's owed.", and the product owner asked that this work be "built for high autonomy".
- The project's memory first, then Garura memory, then Phoenix memory, then research through a memory update a person approves. What nothing answers stays open, with no default. The reason: research is new knowledge that nobody has checked yet, and the person's approval is the check.
