## Intent
- Goal: Before the build of the stories under the item a person names starts, the slice's hidden file holds an entry for each of their features, with each "Fails if" line of the feature's ICE and its capability's ICE, its own id and exactly one recovery, and the slice's evals folder holds a story file for each of those stories, with a scenario for each goal and expectation of the story and for each failure condition of its feature, or an Untestable line with the reason, and the slice file that lists the epic's stories in build order with the surface kind, a time limit and the pass rule.
- Limit: Only the hidden file is written from the "Fails if" lines of the ICE; the evals are written only from the epic with its map of features to stories, the stories under the chosen item and the hidden file, never from an ICE page, the usage guide or the code.
- Limit: Exactly one recovery for each "Fails if" line; never two lines merged and never one split.
- Limit: The way back is guidance, never code, a file name or a command, and when the builder can fix it alone it names what the builder sees and changes in the product, never the evals folder, the eval, the hidden file or the check.
- Limit: Who fixes is "the builder can fix this alone" or "a person must decide", and a person decides when money moves, a person's data reaches the wrong person, an approval is needed, the ICE does not settle a choice, or the step cannot be undone.
- Limit: Every step is a sentence of the step library of the slice's kind of surface, every result is exact, and what only the build will make is a name in square brackets, never a guess.
- Limit: Write only the hidden file, the story files and the slice file in the slice's evals folder, with no way back and no handoff text in an eval; no failure condition or id goes into an epic, a story or the plan file; nothing is kept before the product owner's typed yes on the "Fails if" lines and a summary that never shows the eval, and no copy of the eval or the hidden file stays in short-term memory.
- Fails if: A "Fails if" line of a feature's ICE or its capability's ICE is missing from its entry, or has no recovery or more than one.
- Fails if: A goal or an expectation of a story under the chosen item, or a failure condition of its feature, has no scenario and no Untestable line with its reason.
- Fails if: A step is not a sentence of the library, a result is not exact, or a scenario covers something its story and the hidden file do not say.
- Fails if: A way back holds code, a file name or a command, or names the evals folder, the eval, the hidden file or the check, or an eval holds a way back or a handoff text.
- Fails if: A failure condition or its id is in an epic, a story or the plan file, or in what a part that builds the work receives.
- Fails if: The evals were written from an ICE page, the usage guide or the code, or a file was kept before the product owner's yes.
- Fails if: A slice with no epic, no stories, no hidden file or no step library for its kind of surface got an eval, or the summary did not name it with the reason.

## Context
- Read: Gather it from the memory layer. Look first at the project's existing memory and its existing implementation, then Garura memory, then Phoenix memory. If none of them answers, research it and raise a memory update for a person to approve. For this part, look for: the chosen item and the plan file that holds it, the epic with its map of features to stories, each story's goals, limits, expectations and Context guidance in the tracker, the "Fails if" lines of each feature's ICE and its capability's ICE with their thin marks, the slice and the surface a person tests it on, the rule for a feature's recovery, the eval rules, the two eval templates, the step library of the slice's kind of surface, and the hidden-file entries and eval files the slice already has.
- Never read: the usage guide and the code; and, when the evals are written, any ICE page or the product model.
- Persona: the product owner, who sees the "Fails if" lines, how many the builder can fix alone and how many a person must decide, and a summary of the evals, never the evals, and types yes before anything is kept.
- Scope: In: each feature's entry in the hidden file, the story files and the slice file of the stories under the chosen item, and what the product owner sees. Out: updating an eval or an entry, new step libraries, gathering the work, the ICE and its lines, choosing the slice, running the eval, the handoff plan and the usage guide.

## Expectation
- Scenario: The technical lead of a shop's checkout tool, which runs on the command line, names the story for paying by card. Its feature's ICE says "Fails if: a card is charged twice", and its capability's ICE has one more "Fails if" line. The hidden file's entry holds two failure conditions with their ids; the first gets: when, one order has two charges; way back, refund the second charge; who fixes, a person must decide, because money goes out. The story file holds a scenario for each of the story's goals and expectations and one for each failure condition; one expectation that needs a message from outside the test project goes under Untestable with its reason. The slice file lists the epic's stories in build order. The product owner sees the two lines, the counts and the summary, never the eval, and types yes. No other story file is written, and no story, epic or plan file holds either line.
- Done means: Someone other than the one who wrote the entry compares it with the "Fails if" lines of the two ICE, and finds each line once with exactly one recovery, no code or name of the evals in any way back, and no failure condition in the stories, the epic or the plan file.
- Done means: Someone other than the one who wrote the evals checks the story file against its story and the hidden file, and finds every goal, expectation and failure condition covered by a scenario or named as untestable, every step a sentence of the library, every result exact, and nothing covered that the story and the hidden file do not say.
