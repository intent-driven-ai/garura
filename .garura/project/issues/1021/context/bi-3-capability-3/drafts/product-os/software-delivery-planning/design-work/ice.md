## Intent
- Goal: Every story, chore and spike under the item the technical lead names is designed before it is built, in one design that is the input for writing the code: a technical part with its components, classes, functions, data model, interfaces, libraries and reusable patterns, drawn as diagrams a CTO who is not hands-on can read, and a surface part, what a person sees and does on its one surface and how each screen will look, made against the project's standards from Spec → Design, the approved deployment design among them, with no eval and no test in it.
- Limit: The design starts from the builder's part of the gathered work and each story's collected context; it never reads the test writer's part, the evals folder, an eval, the hidden file or an ICE page, and neither it, its sample code nor its prototype holds an eval, test, success scenario or story expectation.
- Limit: The design follows the project's architecture and technology, its declared surfaces, its approved deployment design and its environments, and never changes them; a need they do not cover is named as a gap for the capability that owns it, and the work goes on.
- Limit: Each choice and each design style comes from the project's code and memory first, then Garura memory, then Phoenix memory; only when none of them answers is it researched, and the research is used only after a person approves it as a memory update. Each choice names its source, the technical lead confirms it, and the sample code, the design style and the screen design follow the project's settings.
- Fails if: An item under the chosen item has no technical part drawn as diagrams, or no surface part that shows each screen as the screen design setting says and no line that says why it has none, or the design covers an item from outside the chosen item's tree.
- Fails if: The design, its sample code or its prototype holds an eval, a test, a success scenario, a story's expectation or a failure condition, or names the evals folder, the hidden file or an ICE page.
- Fails if: The design breaks or changes a project standard, or a choice in it has no source in the project, in memory or in a memory update a person approved.

## Context
- Read: Gather it from the memory layer. Look first at the project's existing memory and its existing implementation, then Garura memory, then Phoenix memory. If none of them answers, research it and raise a memory update for a person to approve. For this part, look for: the builder's part of the gathered tree and each story's collected context, the one surface the work's slice names, the project's architecture with its technology, the declared surface with its properties, guidelines and design system, the approved deployment design with its services, container platform and cloud, the environments, the components, classes, functions, data and patterns already in the project's code, the project's sample code, design style and screen design settings, the styles for drawing a design that memory holds, and the ontology's rules for what a builder reads.
- Never read: the test writer's part, the plan's evals folder, the eval, the slice's hidden file and any ICE page.
- Persona: the technical lead, who designs the work under the chosen item; a CTO reads it from its diagrams, and the developer later builds from it, test first.
- Scope: In: designing every story, chore and spike under the chosen item, technically and for its surface, with its diagrams, its sample code when the setting asks for it and the look of each screen, against the project's standards; naming each gap in the standards and each work item that does not fit; and updating the design as the work changes. Out: choosing and gathering the work, setting or changing the project's standards, how the project runs, where the settings are kept, the evals and the hidden file, the tests, the code and building the application, the fix of a bug, the plan and its work items, the product model, and the ICE itself.

The technical lead confirms the design, and a search of it finds no expectation, eval or test.
