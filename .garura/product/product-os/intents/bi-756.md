# The agent intent (ICE) is its own part of the product model

**Outcome:** the agent intent (ICE) is its own part of the product model. Each ICE has its own page, its own entry in the spine (the file that lists every part and its links), and three parts: Intent, Context and Expectation. A business intent connects to the ICE nodes built from it, not to capabilities.

**Why:** today the ICE is the last section of a capability or feature page, written as six flat labels (Goal, Limit, Fails if, Context, Checked by, Quality need). It has no identity of its own, and its Context and Expectation parts are thin. The Context gives no rules for the sub-agent that gathers context, and the Expectation has no scenarios to build evals from. The review of capability #723 showed this.

**Asked by:** the product owner

**Proof it is met:**
- The ontology has an ICE kind with its own page and spine entry. Each ICE links to one business intent and one capability or feature ("built from" and "is for").
- Each ICE has three parts: Intent (goals, limits, fails if), Context (what the sub-agent reads, what it never reads, the persona, the scope), and Expectation (scenarios, done means, quality needs).
- One ICE exists for each pair of business intent and capability or feature. A second business intent on the same capability gets its own ICE.
- The ready check reads the ICE, and the linter checks its three parts.
- Garura's own model (plans #717, #722 and #734) is moved to the new shape, and no goal or check is lost.

**Must not:** lose any goal, limit, failure or check that the current ICE sections hold.

**Identified by:** #722 — the review of capability #723's ICE showed the ICE cannot live inside a capability page.

**Plan:** this issue's number is the plan's ID — `.garura/project/plans/756.yaml`. Tracking only: a business intent holds no ICE.
