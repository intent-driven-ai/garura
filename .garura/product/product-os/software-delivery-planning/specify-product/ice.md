## Intent
- Goal: What a person gives becomes a confirmed understanding of the product, at the depth the product owner chooses.
- Limit: What a person gives is the input, never the intent. An intent must allow more than one way to build it, so nothing copies how the source does it.
- Limit: Nothing without a basis. Every title, outcome and limit traces to the source, to memory or to the person's own answers.
- Limit: Only a person confirms an intent.
- Limit: Each kind can be written on its own, and no part waits for another to run first.
- Limit: The one who does the work reviews it first, and then a judge with no context checks it. The doer's own review never counts as the check.
- Fails if: A source couldn't be read and the work went on anyway.
- Fails if: An intent describes the source (its screens, fields or copied text) instead of the outcome the person wants.
- Fails if: A value appears that neither the source nor the person gave, with no basis named.
- Fails if: A part of what was given that matters isn't attributed, and the judge can't see that it was read.
- Fails if: Something already in the model is written as new, or a clash between intents isn't shown to a person.

## Context
- Read: Gather it from the memory layer. Look first at the project's existing memory and its existing implementation, then Garura memory, then Phoenix memory. If none of them answers, research it and raise a memory update for a person to approve. For this part, look for: what the person gave, the intents, domains and capabilities already in the model, and what's known about the product's field.
- Persona: the product owner, who wants to confirm what a product is for without writing a spec first.
- Scope: In: keeping what a person gives, walking a prototype in journeys, writing business intents, domains, capabilities and features, and setting quality and compliance needs. Out: linking parts written apart, choosing what to build first, and keeping intents up to date.

## Expectation
- Scenario: A builder who never saw a prototype builds the product from the intents and the model alone, and what a person can do and see on the prototype also works on what they built.
- Done means: After discovery runs on a prototype, `rebuild-eval` tests the result: each intent has its outcome, why, asked by, proof and sources, and the domain is a real one.
- Done means: A judge with clean context gets only what was given and the attribution record, and says what was missed.
- Done means: A person sees each thing sorted, with what it touches.
