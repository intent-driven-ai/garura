## Context

ADR 049 set the shaping loop and gave each slice its own epic and its own eval. ADR 057 set the eval's form: one file for each story, and one slice file. Later records amended both in many places (ADR 052, ADR 063, ADR 067, ADR 068). The ontology (version 16) and /shape-plan still make one Epic for each slice, and /design-eval still writes one eval for each slice (#954).

On 2026-11-12 the product owner said how the parts fit together:

- "we have one epic per plan. 1 plan can have >1 slices."
- "bint -> domain - can have -> capabilities -> features. slices hold features to build and meet the bint. then we make a plan for implementation which can hold 1 or more slices, and also can do chores etc. plan is where product model connects to the work that agents do and track in issue tracker."
- "if eval reads epic, they are reading all plan and all slices already. so its really a matter of 1 eval for epic = plan = multiple slices ... the landing back to main is when epic / plan is done, so eval will need to pass."
- "retire those ADRs and create a new one."

At the accept checkpoint of the first draft, the product owner made six changes. The eval runs by hand until a runner exists (#801). The product model knows no plan and no tracker: "model is about business (intents), and it should not know the agentic (intents) and hence no reference of plans or issue tracker should exist ... the agent (intent) issue tracker or plans should hold back to business intent." The eval's index file is left to the /design-eval rework. The plan file tells whether a slice is in progress. /reshape-plan gets "add a slice". The unplanned epic goes.

At the accept checkpoint of the second draft, the product owner made a second change. This record states the principle and changes no business intent page itself; the change to the Business Intent kind goes through /specify-ontology. A plan's eval covers every feature of its slices. The ids stay as names. And "remove all of them from model. git history is okay": every reference to the tracker and to plans leaves the model, cited issue numbers and the spine's `created_by` and `updated_by` lines included.

At the accept checkpoint of the third draft, the product owner made a third change: "decisions records should not be in the model. that is wrong. so take it out. as for ADRs. the rule stays, older ADRs should be marked retired and new should be recorded." So decision records leave the model, and the one record there is taken out. The orchestrator read "as for ADRs, the rule stays" as: a decision is recorded as an ADR, never in the model.

At the accept checkpoint of the fourth draft, the product owner corrected that reading: "product decisions are recorded in ontology. you ask me questions and i answer. those are product decisions. - this the model part. ADRs represent the agent decisions - this is the code part." So a product decision is the person's answer to a question a play asks, kept in the model as the content of the part it decides. An ADR records a decision about the code. This agrees with the product owner's test of 2026-11-02, on the model's page about keeping ADRs to how the project is built: "an ADR is only for how the project is being developed ... What is written inside the product model is not".

So this record retires ADR 049 and ADR 057 and states, in one place, every part of them that still holds. The rework of /shape-plan for #1025 (PR #1047) and the plan for business intent 1 wait on it. Garura has no plan files today (`.garura/project/plans/` does not exist), so no plan that exists must move.

## For the Person Who Accepts This

The fourth change settled the point on "as for ADRs, the rule stays": a product decision is the content of the part it decides, and an ADR records a decision about the code. One point is open, and two are shown for you to see.

## Decision

### 1. The Plan is where the product model meets the work

A business intent is met through its domains, their capabilities and their features. Slices hold the features to build, to meet the intent. A Plan holds one or more slices of its business intent, plus chores and other work. It is the one place where the product model meets the work that agents do and track in the tracker.

### 2. The product model knows no plan and no tracker

The product model holds no reference to plans, epics, tracker issues or the tracker. The model is about the business; the work is about the agents. The links run one way: from the work side (plans, tracker issues, evals and hidden files) to the model.

- A slice does not name its plan, the plan's folder, its eval, its hidden file or its epic. The plan names its slices.
- The Business Intent kind has no Tracker line on its page. A business intent's What was done says what was delivered and the value it brought, and links to no work item.
- No page cites a tracker issue, in its lines or in its Sources. No spine entry holds an issue number. The spine's `created_by` and `updated_by` name the play or the agent and what it did, never an issue. The spine has no `epics` list, and no part of the model holds a work status.
- Where a part came from is kept in git history. Git history is not changed.
- The ids that exist (`bi-<n>`, `fn-<n>`) stay as names. A new business intent gets an id that does not come from the tracker; that is later work.
- A part whose subject is plans may still describe plans in general (ADR 065 rule 3).

Decision records leave the product model too, and each decision has one home by what it is about:

- No decision record lives in the model. No `decision.yaml` record sits in it, and no part links to one: the spine's `decisions` links go.
- The one record in Garura's model, `.garura/product/product-os/decisions/dec-cap-initialize-security-none-medium.yaml`, is taken out whole. It is not edited first, so the rule that an accepted record is never edited holds. Git history keeps it.
- **A product decision** is the person's answer to a question that a play asks them. It is kept in the product model, as the content of the part it decides, in the place the ontology gives that part: for example, the product profile's security level and its gate, or a business intent's proof. It is not a separate record.
- **An ADR records a decision about the code:** how the agents and the plays work, and how the product is built. ADRs stay in the project's ADR folder (`docs/adr/` for Garura), in Markdown. The ADR template keeps one shape, Markdown; its YAML shape and the decision schema go.
- The ADR rule stays: an accepted ADR is never edited; an older ADR that a new one replaces is marked superseded by a forward pointer; each new decision is a new ADR.
- A box-move (a raised product limit) still stops for the product owner's yes. The answer is kept on the profile itself, as the raised level and its gate. The reason it moved is kept in history: the change that lands it, in git. It writes no decision record and no ADR.

This record states the principle. It changes no business intent page itself. The ontology's Business Intent kind changes through /specify-ontology, at the product owner's checkpoints, and the clean-up of the model follows it (Work This Creates). No business intent's title, outcome, why, asked by, proof, must not, rules or stage changes.

### 3. One Epic for each plan

A plan has one Epic. It holds the Feature issues of all the plan's slices (ADR 068), with their Stories inside them. Its feature-to-story map has one line for each feature of each slice the plan holds. A plan's chores sit in the plan, under its one epic. A bug still sits under the Story that introduced it.

### 4. One eval for each plan, and a done plan lands only when it passes

/design-eval reads the plan's epic: its Feature issues' scope of work and Context guidance, and the hidden file of each slice. It writes one eval for the plan, in the plan's evals folder. The eval covers every feature of every slice the plan holds, also a feature whose Feature issue sits under another plan's epic (`held_by`). When two plans hold one slice, each plan's eval tests its features. The plan lands on main as done only when that eval passes. Until an eval runner exists, the Garura harness runs the plan's eval by hand before a done plan lands; the runner is #801. A paused plan still merges into main, as ADR 060 §2 says. What replaces the slice file as the eval's index, and how the eval and the usage guides are laid out, is left to the /design-eval rework.

### 5. The plan file tells whether a slice is in progress

The plan file records each slice it holds and that slice's status. A slice is in progress when the plan file records it so. When two plans hold one slice, each plan file records the slice's status for itself. /design-slice and /reshape-plan read it there; the model is not asked.

### 6. A plan that exists gains a slice through /reshape-plan

/reshape-plan gets an "add a slice" instruction for a plan that exists. The slice's Feature issues go under the plan's one epic, and the plan file records the slice. /shape-plan still makes a plan only once (ADR 063 §1).

### 7. The unplanned epic goes

There is no unplanned epic. The work it gathered sits in the business intent's plan, under the plan's one epic.

### 8. What goes

- One Epic for each slice (ADR 049 §1.2; ADR 048 §1 and §3).
- One epic shared by two plans when a slice serves two intents (ADR 049 §2).
- One eval for each slice (ADR 049 §1.3 and §3; ADR 057 §1).
- The slice file `eval.<slice-id>.md` as the index of one slice's eval (ADR 057 §3).
- The Tracker line on a business intent page (ADR 068 §1), through the change to the Business Intent kind.
- Tracker issue numbers in the model: cited in a page or its Sources, in a spine entry's `issue` field, and in the spine's `created_by` and `updated_by` lines.
- "A slice is in progress when its epic is past ready" (ADR 058 §3, ADR 062 §2).
- The spine's `epics` list (ADR 048 §5).
- The unplanned epic (`managing-work.md`; no ADR recorded it).
- Decision records in the model: the `decisions/` folder, the spine's `decisions` links, the decision schema (`decision.yaml`) and the ADR template's YAML shape (ADR 026 "Containment split", ADR 032 §2 and §7, ADR 033 §1 and §5; the schema came with #434 and the shape with #632).

### 9. What still holds from ADR 049, as amended

1. **The shaping loop.** It runs /design-slice, then /shape-plan, then /design-eval. /design-slice shapes one slice and writes the link "slice serves business intent", a thin ICE for each part the slice needs that has none, and the slice's hidden file. It writes no plan, no epic, no Feature issue, no story and no eval. A thing with no surface that a person can test end to end is not a slice. /shape-plan runs right after it, with no stop between them, and makes the plan once (ADR 063): the plan file, the plan's epic, and one Feature issue for each feature that is not yet built, found or filed once (ADR 068). The plan does not pick, rank or test slices; the person chose the slice at /design-slice.
2. **One plan for each business intent.** A business intent has one plan, and never a second. The plan's ID is the intent's issue number. The plan holds every slice that serves its intent, read from the link "slice serves business intent". A plan that exists is changed by /reshape-plan, or deleted whole on a person's named approval (ADR 063, ADR 065).
3. **Where the files live.** A plan's folder, `.garura/project/plans/<plan-id>/`, holds `evals/` and `guides/`. Its evals folder holds, in `evals/<slug>/`, the hidden file `failure-and-recovery.md` of each slice it holds that was first shaped for its own intent. A plan that holds a slice first shaped for another intent points to that plan's file and never copies it. The plays work this out from the plan and the slice's first intent; nothing in the model stores it. The build writes the usage guide in the plan's guides folder, beside the evals folder, not in it. The guide is the eval's only way into the built product.
4. **How the ICE reaches the work, the eval and the builder.**
   - /shape-plan reads every line of the feature's ICE and its capability's ICE except the "Fails if" lines. It writes the Feature issue's scope of work (the goals, the limits and the expectations) and its Context guidance (what to read, what never to read, the persona, the scope). The Feature issue never holds a failure condition, a recovery condition or the context itself.
   - The build play's context sub-agent reads the Feature issue's Context guidance and collects the context into the issue's short-term memory, `.garura/project/issues/<issue>/`. It never opens an ICE page or a plan's evals folder. The builder reads the Feature issue and that context.
   - /design-slice writes the hidden file from the "Fails if" lines of the same two ICE: for each feature, its failure conditions, and exactly one recovery condition for each (when, way back, who fixes).
   - The eval is read at two times. The eval play, before the build, reads the plan's epic, its Feature issues and the hidden files, never the usage guide or the code. The running eval, after the build, reads the eval, the usage guide, the Context guidance in the eval and only the recovery parts of the hidden file, never the failure conditions.
   - A failure gives a handoff plan: what the product does wrong, as a symptom, and the way back. It never names the failure condition, its id or the check. It goes to the builder or to a person, as the recovery condition says.
   - When an ICE changes after its Feature issue is made, the Feature issue and its hidden-file entry are drift. In the same Alignment round, /shape-plan makes the Feature issue again and /design-slice makes the entry again, from the same two ICE.
   - The read rule is the whole guard: no builder, no skill that builds the software and no context sub-agent reads a plan's evals folder.
5. **The recovery rule covers a feature.** `core/components/memory/standards/rules/expectation-generation.md`, "A feature's recovery (product ICE)": one recovery condition for each "Fails if" line, marked "the builder can fix this alone" or "a person must decide".

### 10. What still holds from ADR 057, as amended

1. **Plain text that runs with no agent.** The eval is plain-text Markdown with Gherkin. It is not encrypted. It runs later, with no agent.
2. **One file for each Feature issue.** The eval has one file for each Feature issue in the plan's epic, `eval.<feature-id>.feature.md`. Its Gherkin `Feature:` is the Feature issue. It holds one scenario for each goal and each expectation of the Feature issue and, for each failure condition of its feature in the hidden file, a scenario that shows the failure does not happen. Each scenario is tagged `@goal-<k>`, `@expectation-<k>` or `@F<n>`. What cannot be tested yet is listed under "Untestable", with its reason.
3. **Deterministic.** Every step is a sentence from the fixed step library of the slice's surface kind (`standards/schemas/eval/steps-<kind>.yaml`). Every Then is an exact result. There is no rubric and no score: the eval passes only when every scenario passes. What only the build will make is a name in square brackets, which the usage guide maps. Each scenario has a time limit.
4. **A fixed yardstick.** A later run of /design-eval only adds scenarios. It never changes or deletes one.
5. **A summary, never the eval.** At the checkpoint, the product owner sees the counts for each Feature issue and the untestable items, never the eval itself.

## Consequences

### Positive

- The plan, its epic and its eval are one unit, and the plan lands as one. A person sees all the work for one intent under one epic.
- The plan's tree no longer splits into several epics when the plan holds several slices.
- The model does not go out of date when a plan, an epic or an issue changes, because it names none of them. Each link has one side to keep.
- Whether a slice is in progress is told for each slice, not for a whole epic.
- A second slice can join a plan that exists.
- The parts of ADR 049 and ADR 057 that still hold, with five later amendments applied, are in one record.
- A done plan cannot land on main with an eval that fails.
- The model holds no decision record, and an ADR has one shape and one home.
- Each decision has one home by what it is about: a product decision is in the part it decides, and a decision about the code is an ADR.

### Negative / Risks

- Until #801 exists, "the eval passes" rests on a run by hand, which nothing records or checks.
- When two plans hold one slice, both evals test the same `held_by` features, and the two plan files can record different statuses for it.
- The ids `bi-<n>` and `fn-<n>` still carry their tracker numbers, as names.
- To learn where a part came from, a person reads git history; the page no longer says it. The clean-up changes 271 pages, 559 metadata lines and 23 spine fields in Garura's own model.
- The reason for a box-move, and for any product decision, is kept only in git history. A person reads the change that landed it to learn why; the profile shows only the level and its gate.
- "Agent decision" names two things until the first point above is decided.
- The ADR template, author-decision, both copies of `adr.py`, /design-adr and adr-keeper lose the YAML shape. A project that has decision records of its own in its model must move them out; Garura has one.
- link-model-part must change before the spine can be cleaned.
- A slice's status in the plan file is set by a person, so it can be wrong when no one updates it.
- A person can no longer go from an intent page to its tracker issue in one click.
- /shape-plan (with PR #1047), author-work-items, /design-eval, author-slice-eval, evals-engineer, the eval templates, /reshape-plan, author-plan-changes, /design-slice, merge-work, author-intent-page, link-model-part, the plan tool and the ontology all change before the next plan is made.
- Until the ontology is promoted to Phoenix memory (version 15 there), agents that read it through the memory tool still read one Epic for each Slice.

## Alternatives Considered

- **One Epic for each slice (ADR 049 as it is)** — lost: a plan with several slices then has several epics, and the plan's tree splits.
- **One eval for each slice (ADR 057 as it is)** — lost: several evals for one landing, while the plan lands as one.
- **Amend ADR 049 and ADR 057 instead of retiring them** — lost: the product owner chose to retire them and restate what still holds in one record.
- **Keep the model's links to plans and the tracker (the Tracker line, a slice's eval and home plan, the spine's `epics`)** — lost: a link kept on both sides drifts, and the model is about the business, not the agents' work.
- **Keep cited issue numbers and the spine's `created_by` and `updated_by` issue numbers as sources** — lost: the product owner chose to remove them; git history keeps where each part came from.
- **Change the intent pages in this record** — lost: the product owner chose that this record states the principle, and that the Business Intent kind changes through /specify-ontology, with the product owner's checkpoints.
- **Tell a slice in progress from the plan's epic** — lost: one epic for each plan cannot tell slices apart.
- **Keep the unplanned epic as a second, marked epic** — lost: it breaks one epic for each plan.
- **Keep product decision records in the model, with `decided_in` naming a run** — lost: the product owner said that decision records should not be in the model.
- **Record every decision, a product decision included, as an ADR in `docs/adr/` (the third draft's reading)** — lost: the product owner split them; a product decision is the person's answer in the model, and ADRs are for the code.
- **Keep a Product Decision kind in the ontology (the earlier #1021 answer)** — lost: a product decision is the content of the part it decides, not a record, so a kind of its own has nothing to define.
