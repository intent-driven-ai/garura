# Intent Depth

How deep business intents go when a person gives something to work from. The aim is the right intents, not many intents.

## The rule

- **One need is one intent.** A source can hold many needs, so it can give many intents.
- **An intent can be big.** The detail goes in its features. A user checking out a cart by giving their details and applying a discount can be one intent, with the discount as a feature.
- **The product owner chooses.** When a part could be one intent or two, the play shows both and the product owner decides. They can also set a preference for smaller or bigger intents.
- **Not every part is an intent.** Parts that only connect others are transitional. They are recorded as considered, with the reason they are not an intent.
- **Every part is considered.** Coverage means each part that matters was looked at, not that each one became something.

## What goes wrong

- **An intent for every small part.** That gives far too many, and no one can hold them in mind.
- **A part left out with no reason.** Nobody can tell if it was missed or judged.
- **Writing as new what already exists.** A source may enhance an intent, add a feature under one, or clash with one. Read what exists first.

## Examples

- **Shop.** A video shows adding to a cart, checking out, and recommendations. These are three needs. The screen between the cart and checkout is transitional.
- **Clinic.** A document describes booking, and moving a booking. The product owner may take one intent with two features, or two intents.
- **Bank.** A prototype shows a loan form and a help link. The form is a need. The help link may be a feature, or nothing at all.
